# Guernsey–UK: residence, employment, investment income and relief

## Who this applies to

The Agreement applies to residents of one or both territories. Article 3 defines geographical Guernsey as **Guernsey, Alderney and Herm**, with adjacent territorial sea; it does not list Sark. Do not equate the treaty definition with every place in the Bailiwick. Article 2 identifies Guernsey income tax and UK income tax, corporation tax and capital gains tax. This Guide does not establish social-security, immigration, VAT or inheritance-tax relief. [Articles 1–3](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c470999ed915d38b0437c54/2018_Guernsey-UK_Double_Taxation_Agreement_in_force.pdf).

Article 4 residence requires liability by domicile, residence, management, incorporation or a similar criterion; source-only taxation does not suffice. Establish domestic residence first, using the [individual Guide](gg-individual-income-tax.md) and appropriate UK rules. Transparent entities need Article 1(2)'s income/resident attribution test. The Protocol supplies additional Article 4 interpretation, including a specified Guernsey exempt investment-fund provision; do not infer that a zero liability alone proves either eligibility or ineligibility. [Articles 1 and 4; Protocol paragraph 2](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c470999ed915d38b0437c54/2018_Guernsey-UK_Double_Taxation_Agreement_in_force.pdf).

The Agreement entered into force on **7 January 2019**. Ordinary effective dates are **1 March 2019** for withholding in both territories, **1 January 2020** for Guernsey income tax, **6 April 2019** for UK income tax and **1 April 2019** for UK corporation tax. Historical transactions require the appropriate earlier agreement. HMRC currently identifies this Agreement as in force. [Treaty-status page](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/guernsey-tax-treaties); [DT8600](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/double-taxation-relief/dt8600).

## Residence conflicts

For an individual resident in both territories under Article 4(1), apply Article 4(2) in sequence:

1. Permanent home available; if available in both, centre of vital interests (closer personal/economic relations).
2. If that centre cannot be determined, or neither territory has an available permanent home, habitual abode.
3. Habitual abode in both or neither: competent-authority mutual agreement.

There is **no nationality tie-break step** in this treaty's individual rule. Retain home availability, family/economic ties and living-pattern evidence rather than treating a passport or one year's day count as decisive. [Article 4(2)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c470999ed915d38b0437c54/2018_Guernsey-UK_Double_Taxation_Agreement_in_force.pdf).

For a dual-resident non-individual, Article 4(3) calls for competent-authority agreement taking account of effective management, incorporation/constitution and other factors. Without agreement, treaty benefits are unavailable except those under Articles **22, 24 and 25**. Do not apply an automatic effective-management tie-break or deny every benefit indiscriminately. [Article 4(3)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c470999ed915d38b0437c54/2018_Guernsey-UK_Double_Taxation_Agreement_in_force.pdf).

## Business profits and permanent establishments

Article 7 generally reserves enterprise profits to its residence territory unless business is carried on through a permanent establishment (PE) in the other territory; that other territory can tax profits attributable to the PE. Establish treaty residence and entitlement before this allocation. A treaty restriction is not itself proof of a domestic exemption or a substitute for domestic filing obligations. [Article 7(1)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c470999ed915d38b0437c54/2018_Guernsey-UK_Double_Taxation_Agreement_in_force.pdf).

Article 5(1) defines a PE as a fixed place of business through which the enterprise's business is wholly or partly carried on. Listed examples include management premises, branches, offices, factories, workshops and extraction sites. Assess actual premises use and activity rather than concluding from a registered address or customer location alone. [Article 5(1)–(2)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c470999ed915d38b0437c54/2018_Guernsey-UK_Double_Taxation_Agreement_in_force.pdf).

| Issue | Treaty rule and evidence |
|---|---|
| Building/construction/installation project | A PE under Article 5(3) only if it lasts **more than twelve months**. Establish commencement, completion, interruptions and project identity; do not substitute a fixed 365-day test without authority. |
| Limited activities | Article 5(4) lists facilities/stock solely for storage, display or delivery, stock solely for processing by another enterprise, and a place solely for purchasing or collecting information. Its other-activity limb requires preparatory/auxiliary character; combined listed activities require that character overall. Read the exact limb rather than importing a different treaty's wording. |
| Fragmented operations | Article 5(5) can disapply the paragraph 4 exception for complementary functions forming a cohesive business operation, where the same/closely related enterprise operates at the same or another place and the specified PE or non-auxiliary combined-activity condition is met. |
| Contract-concluding agent | Article 5(7) covers a person acting for the enterprise who has and habitually exercises authority to conclude contracts on its behalf, subject to its stated exclusions. Contract practice and authority matter, not just job title. |
| Independent agent | Article 5(8) protects business through an independent-status broker/commission agent/other agent acting in the ordinary course of its business. Verify both independence and ordinary-course conduct. |
| Parent/subsidiary | Control alone does not make one company a PE of the other under Article 5(9). Other fixed-place or agency facts still need assessment. |

All rules: [Article 5(3)–(9)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c470999ed915d38b0437c54/2018_Guernsey-UK_Double_Taxation_Agreement_in_force.pdf). For paragraph 5, Article 5(6) defines close relationship by factual control and adds a **more-than-50%** direct/indirect beneficial-interest test, with the specified vote/value/equity measures for companies. Exactly 50% fails that automatic percentage limb but is not a safe harbour from factual control. Do not apply Article 5(6)'s definition as a universal threshold for Article 9 or the investment-income articles.

### Attribute profit from evidence, not a fixed turnover fraction

Article 7(2) treats the PE as a separate independent enterprise in the same/similar activities and conditions, considering functions performed, assets used and risks assumed through it and the rest of the enterprise. Prepare a supported attribution calculation, including internal dealings; neither all worldwide profit nor an arbitrary share of turnover is automatically the PE base. Article 7(3) provides an appropriate corresponding tax adjustment where an attribution adjustment causes the specified double taxation, with authority consultation if needed. [Article 7(2)–(3)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c470999ed915d38b0437c54/2018_Guernsey-UK_Double_Taxation_Agreement_in_force.pdf).

Other articles continue to govern separately covered income/gains under Article 7(4). For example, Article 6 permits the property territory to tax income from immovable property, including enterprise property and letting income; absence of an Article 5 PE does not by itself exempt that rent. [Articles 6 and 7(4)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c470999ed915d38b0437c54/2018_Guernsey-UK_Double_Taxation_Agreement_in_force.pdf).

**Ownership boundary example:** assume 100 equal beneficial interests with no special rights or indirect interests. Holding 50 is 50%; holding 51 is 51%. Only the latter necessarily meets the more-than-50% numerical limb. Both still require the paragraph 5 business facts, and the first can be closely related through factual control. The arithmetic does not decide a PE.

```oa-check
name: PE anti-fragmentation ownership boundary
inputs:
  total_equal_interests: 100
  at_boundary: 50
  above_boundary: 51
steps:
  - boundary_percentage: at_boundary / total_equal_interests * 100
  - above_percentage: above_boundary / total_equal_interests * 100
expected:
  boundary_percentage: 50
  above_percentage: 51
```

**Attribution working-paper illustration:** assume a separately supported Article 7 analysis attributes £200,000 revenue and £130,000 allowable costs to a PE. Attributed profit is **£70,000**. If review of an internal dealing establishes an additional £10,000 attributable profit adjustment, the revised base is **£80,000**. These are hypothetical evidence inputs, not a treaty-prescribed margin, cost deduction or tax rate. Apply domestic tax law and corresponding-adjustment procedures separately.

```oa-check
name: Supported PE profit bridge before domestic tax
inputs:
  attributed_revenue: 200000
  established_allowable_costs: 130000
  supported_profit_adjustment: 10000
steps:
  - initial_profit: attributed_revenue - established_allowable_costs
  - revised_profit: initial_profit + supported_profit_adjustment
expected:
  initial_profit: 70000
  revised_profit: 80000
```

Unresolved: detailed project aggregation/timing, remote-worker premises analysis, agency interpretation, domestic PE registration/returns, branch deductions and current competent-authority procedures. The examples do not settle these points.

## Employment allocation and thresholds

Ordinary employment remuneration is taxable only in the residence territory unless the employment is exercised in the other territory, in which case the latter can tax the remuneration derived from that work. Directors' fees, pensions and government service have separate Articles 15, 17 and 18, to which Article 14 is subject. [Article 14(1)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c470999ed915d38b0437c54/2018_Guernsey-UK_Double_Taxation_Agreement_in_force.pdf).

For work exercised in the other territory, Article 14(2) preserves residence-only taxation only if **all three** conditions hold:

| Condition | Evidence needed |
|---|---|
| Presence does not exceed 183 days in any twelve-month period commencing or ending in the fiscal year concerned | Travel record and rolling-window calculation; not calendar-year or workday counting alone |
| Employer paying, or on whose behalf pay is made, is not resident in the work territory | Employer identity, residence and employment/assignment arrangements |
| Remuneration is not borne by the employer's permanent establishment there | Cost allocation, recharge and permanent-establishment analysis |

Source: [Article 14(2)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c470999ed915d38b0437c54/2018_Guernsey-UK_Double_Taxation_Agreement_in_force.pdf). Exactly 183 satisfies the numeric limb; 184 does not. Satisfying that limb does not settle the employer or cost-bearing conditions. The detailed domestic presence-count conventions and economic-employer interpretation require verification for the actual assignment. Article 14(3) separately addresses qualifying international ship/aircraft employment.

## The base and Guernsey credit ceiling

For a Guernsey resident's income that the UK can tax under the Agreement, Article 22(1)(a) provides credit for UK income tax paid, subject to Guernsey credit law. Paragraph (b) caps it at the Guernsey tax attributable to that income **before the credit**. Confirm identical income, periods, eligible tax and the treaty's UK taxing right; a payroll deduction is not by itself proof that all tax withheld is treaty-permitted. [Article 22(1)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c470999ed915d38b0437c54/2018_Guernsey-UK_Double_Taxation_Agreement_in_force.pdf).

Where income is taxable only in the UK under the treaty, Article 22(1)(c) instead allows Guernsey to include it when calculating tax on remaining income. Article 22(4) excludes the paragraph 1 credit where UK taxation is permitted solely because the income is also derived by a UK resident. Do not turn a dual-residence fact into an automatic credit. UK-side relief follows Article 22(2) and UK law; this Guide does not compute it. [Article 22(1)–(5)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c470999ed915d38b0437c54/2018_Guernsey-UK_Double_Taxation_Agreement_in_force.pdf).

Article 1(3) preserves residence-territory taxation subject to its listed exceptions, including Article 22. Article 23 can deny a benefit when obtaining it was a principal purpose, unless granting it accords with the relevant provisions' object and purpose; paragraph 2 provides a competent-authority request route. A residence certificate does not displace those rules. [Articles 1(3) and 23](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c470999ed915d38b0437c54/2018_Guernsey-UK_Double_Taxation_Agreement_in_force.pdf).

## Dividends, interest and royalties

Treaty limits constrain the source territory's permitted tax; they do not create a domestic charge or determine the residence territory's tax. Establish the domestic treatment, income category, beneficial owner, treaty residence and Article 23 entitlement before calculating relief. HMRC's summary expressly distinguishes treaty rates from domestic rates. [Articles 10–12](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c470999ed915d38b0437c54/2018_Guernsey-UK_Double_Taxation_Agreement_in_force.pdf); [DT8602](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/double-taxation-relief/dt8602).

### Dividends

Article 10(2)(a) generally exempts a dividend from source-territory tax where its beneficial owner is resident in the other territory, subject to the article's exceptions. It does not set a minimum ownership percentage for this ordinary exemption. Residence-territory taxation and tax on the distributing company's profits remain separate. [Article 10(1)–(3)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c470999ed915d38b0437c54/2018_Guernsey-UK_Double_Taxation_Agreement_in_force.pdf).

The exception in Article 10(2)(b) concerns dividends from an investment vehicle's directly or indirectly derived immovable-property income/gains where the vehicle distributes most of that income annually and the property income is exempt from tax. Source tax is capped at **15% of the gross dividend**. A pension scheme established in the other territory retains the paragraph (a) exemption. Do not apply 15% merely because a company owns a building; establish the vehicle and distribution conditions. [Article 10(2)(b)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c470999ed915d38b0437c54/2018_Guernsey-UK_Double_Taxation_Agreement_in_force.pdf).

If the holding is effectively connected with the beneficial owner's permanent establishment in the payer's territory, Article 10(4) refers the matter to Article 7 instead. This is an asset-to-establishment connection test, not simply whether the shareholder has any business presence there. [Article 10(4)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c470999ed915d38b0437c54/2018_Guernsey-UK_Double_Taxation_Agreement_in_force.pdf).

**Ceiling example:** for an established qualifying property-income dividend of £10,000, the treaty ceiling is **£1,500**. If independently verified domestic source tax would be £2,000, the treaty-limited amount is £1,500, subject to claim procedures. If domestic tax instead is £1,000, the treaty does not raise it to £1,500. No domestic rate is asserted by these assumed figures.

```oa-check
name: Property-income dividend treaty ceiling is not a minimum tax
inputs:
  gross_dividend: 10000
  ceiling_rate: 0.15
  higher_domestic_tax: 2000
  lower_domestic_tax: 1000
steps:
  - ceiling: gross_dividend * ceiling_rate
  - higher_case_tax: min(higher_domestic_tax, ceiling)
  - lower_case_tax: min(lower_domestic_tax, ceiling)
expected:
  ceiling: 1500
  higher_case_tax: 1500
  lower_case_tax: 1000
```

### Interest and royalties: qualifying owners differ

Articles 11(2) and 12(2) allow source taxation under domestic law unless at least one of their respective paragraph 3 conditions grants residence-only taxation. They do **not** supply a general reduced percentage for every non-qualifying recipient. For both categories, qualifying routes include an individual, a company with substantial regular trading in its principal share class on a recognised exchange, a company with **less than 25%** of shares/other rights owned directly or indirectly by non-residents of the recipient's territory, and specified public bodies. A further route requires the competent authority's determination regarding the purpose of establishing/acquiring/maintaining the recipient or conducting its operations. Do not replace that determination with an internal assurance. [Articles 11(2)–(4), 12(2)–(4)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c470999ed915d38b0437c54/2018_Guernsey-UK_Double_Taxation_Agreement_in_force.pdf).

Interest has additional express routes, including a pension scheme, bank/building society, the defined unrelated independently dealing financial institution, and specified public-sector payers. Those additional categories are not all repeated in the royalty article. A bank or pension label alone therefore does not establish royalty relief through the interest provision. Verify the exact recipient category under the correct article. Recognised exchanges are specified in Protocol paragraph 3; not every trading platform qualifies. [Article 11(3); Article 12(3); Protocol paragraph 3](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c470999ed915d38b0437c54/2018_Guernsey-UK_Double_Taxation_Agreement_in_force.pdf).

For the ownership route, a company with 24% non-resident ownership passes the numeric limb; exactly 25% does not. Count direct and indirect shares or other rights using supported ownership records. Failure of this route does not rule out another paragraph 3 route. For example, 24 of 100 equal shares represent 24%, while 25 represent 25%; no alternative rights or indirect holdings are assumed in this illustration.

```oa-check
name: Non-resident ownership strict less-than boundary
inputs:
  total_equal_shares: 100
  below_boundary_shares: 24
  boundary_shares: 25
steps:
  - below_percentage: below_boundary_shares / total_equal_shares * 100
  - boundary_percentage: boundary_shares / total_equal_shares * 100
expected:
  below_percentage: 24
  boundary_percentage: 25
```

Interest and royalty source is generally the payer's residence, subject to the relevant permanent-establishment liability/cost-bearing provisions in paragraph 7. Effectively connected debt claims or IP rights require the paragraph 6/Article 7 analysis. Article 12(6)'s published wording refers specifically to paragraph 1; its interaction with paragraph 2 needs case-specific review rather than silently rewriting the treaty. The excessive related-party amount restrictions in paragraph 8 are addressed below. Classification, beneficial ownership, permanent-establishment connection and claim clearance are not established by a payment description alone. [Articles 11(5)–(8), 12(5)–(8)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c470999ed915d38b0437c54/2018_Guernsey-UK_Double_Taxation_Agreement_in_force.pdf).

## Associated enterprises and corresponding adjustments

Article 9(1) applies where one territory's enterprise participates directly or indirectly in the management, control or capital of the other territory's enterprise, or the same persons participate in both. If their commercial or financial conditions differ from independent-enterprise conditions, profits that would otherwise have accrued can be included and taxed. The article supplies no numeric ownership-percentage safe harbour; identify management/control/capital facts rather than inventing a minimum shareholding. [Article 9(1)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c470999ed915d38b0437c54/2018_Guernsey-UK_Double_Taxation_Agreement_in_force.pdf).

Article 9(2) addresses resulting double taxation: where one territory includes and taxes profits already taxed in the other, and those profits satisfy the independent-enterprise condition, the other territory must make an appropriate adjustment to its tax on those profits, considering the Agreement. Authorities consult if necessary. This is not a right to copy a foreign adjustment into a return without establishing its basis, nor a promise that both territories' tax changes will be numerically equal. The domestic claim procedure, evidence standard and deadlines remain to be verified; preserve the Article 25 dispute deadline separately. [Article 9(2)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c470999ed915d38b0437c54/2018_Guernsey-UK_Double_Taxation_Agreement_in_force.pdf).

**Recommended evidence:** identify the transaction and both enterprises, ownership/control links, contracts and actual conduct, the pricing method and comparables, each territory's original profit and tax computations, the assessment adjustment and the amount of overlapping profit. These are working-paper controls, not a verified Guernsey statutory master-file/local-file requirement. Domestic anti-avoidance and deduction rules still need primary-source verification before this constitutes a Guernsey transfer-pricing Guide.

Articles 11(8) and 12(8) separately limit their interest/royalty provisions to the amount that would have been agreed without the stated special relationship. The excess remains subject to domestic law with regard to the Agreement's other provisions. Do not apply an otherwise available treaty exemption to the entire related-party payment without testing the amount. No blanket withholding rate is established by this section. [Articles 11(8) and 12(8)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c470999ed915d38b0437c54/2018_Guernsey-UK_Double_Taxation_Agreement_in_force.pdf).

**Adjustment illustration:** assume an accepted pricing analysis establishes independent-enterprise remuneration of £120,000 for a service priced at £90,000 and that the Article 9 conditions are met. The profit adjustment under review is **£30,000**. If the same profit was already included in the counterparty's tax base, identify that overlap for the corresponding-adjustment request; do not treat £30,000 as the tax refund. Likewise, if a £50,000 related-party interest payment has an established independent amount of £40,000, separate the **£10,000 excess** before testing the relevant treaty article. The benchmark figures are assumed evidence, not published safe-harbour prices or rates.

```oa-check
name: Associated-enterprise profit adjustment and excess interest
inputs:
  supported_service_price: 120000
  actual_service_price: 90000
  actual_interest: 50000
  supported_independent_interest: 40000
steps:
  - profit_adjustment: supported_service_price - actual_service_price
  - excess_interest: actual_interest - supported_independent_interest
expected:
  profit_adjustment: 30000
  excess_interest: 10000
```

## Filing, deadlines and disputes

Prepare the domestic returns and relief claims for each applicable year; retain residence evidence, employment-location records, payslips, assessments, proof of tax paid and credit allocation. Exact claim forms, deadlines, payroll clearance and treatment of later foreign refunds remain unverified. Do not stop withholding solely because this working paper identifies potential treaty relief.

Under Article 25(1), a person can present a treaty-inconsistent taxation case to either competent authority within **three years from first notification of the action** producing that taxation, irrespective of domestic remedies. Record the actual notification and service evidence. Article 25 does not replace domestic appeal or payment deadlines; its competent-authority process is not a promise of immediate refund or suspension. Arbitration has further conditions and separate implementation material; this Guide does not establish that route's current availability. [Article 25](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5c470999ed915d38b0437c54/2018_Guernsey-UK_Double_Taxation_Agreement_in_force.pdf).

## Penalties and interest

No treaty penalty or interest rate is calculated here. Determine domestic late-return, late-payment and inaccurate-claim consequences separately. A treaty claim or mutual-agreement request is not assumed to suspend collection. Keep each territory's filing and payment calendar while seeking relief.

## Two worked examples that recompute

**1. The 183-day boundary is only one limb.** Assume properly counted presence blocks of 90 and 93 days within a tested twelve-month window, with all other relevant windows checked. The total is **183**. One additional day gives **184** and fails the numeric condition. In the 183-day case, residence-only treatment still requires the employer and permanent-establishment conditions; a work-territory employer would defeat the exception even at 90 days.

```oa-check
name: Employment presence boundary in one tested rolling window
inputs:
  first_visit_days: 90
  second_visit_days: 93
  additional_day: 1
steps:
  - at_boundary: first_visit_days + second_visit_days
  - over_boundary: at_boundary + additional_day
expected:
  at_boundary: 183
  over_boundary: 184
```

**2. Credit is capped by tax on the same income.** Assume treaty eligibility is established, UK tax of £3,000 is properly payable and paid on the relevant income, and the separately verified Guernsey pre-credit tax on that income is £4,000. Credit is £3,000 and residual Guernsey tax is **£1,000**. If eligible UK tax instead is £5,000, the credit is capped at £4,000 and residual Guernsey tax is **£0**. The excess £1,000 is not a Guernsey refund generated by this formula. Both tax amounts are assumed inputs; no domestic rate, income allocation or allowance is inferred.

```oa-check
name: Guernsey treaty credit below and above the same-income ceiling
inputs:
  guernsey_precredit_tax: 4000
  lower_eligible_uk_tax: 3000
  higher_eligible_uk_tax: 5000
steps:
  - lower_credit: min(guernsey_precredit_tax, lower_eligible_uk_tax)
  - lower_residual: guernsey_precredit_tax - lower_credit
  - higher_credit: min(guernsey_precredit_tax, higher_eligible_uk_tax)
  - higher_residual: guernsey_precredit_tax - higher_credit
  - excess_over_ceiling: higher_eligible_uk_tax - higher_credit
expected:
  lower_credit: 3000
  lower_residual: 1000
  higher_credit: 4000
  higher_residual: 0
  excess_over_ceiling: 1000
```

## Ask the client first

1. Where is the taxpayer domestically resident, and what home/ties/history evidence resolves any dual residence?
2. Where was employment exercised, on what dates, and across which rolling twelve-month windows?
3. Who is the employer, where is it resident, and which entity or establishment bears the cost?
4. What income and tax do the assessments/payment records cover, and has either authority made an adjustment or refund?
5. When was any disputed taxation first notified, and which domestic appeal/claim deadlines remain open?
