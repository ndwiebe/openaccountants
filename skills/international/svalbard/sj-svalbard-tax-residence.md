# Svalbard: residence and cross-border tax in 2026 v0.2

> **Source-cited draft.** Tax year 2026, checked 24 October 2026. General reference for working papers; a qualified professional must review the facts and filing position. Independent AI review does not confer accountant review.

## Who this applies to

This is the pack's **XB** guide. It concerns Svalbard within [Svalbard Tax Act §1-1](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1996-11-29-68); the shared SJ catalogue code does not extend it to Jan Mayen. Apply residence, source and the relevant income category separately.

An individual taking permanent residence is resident. A stay lasting **at least 12 months** counts as residence **from the beginning**, not from month 13 (§2-1). Real residential attachment is factual; a brief holiday/business absence does not necessarily break it ([residence guidance](https://www.skatteetaten.no/person/skatt/hjelp-til-riktig-skatt/svalbard/hvem-er-skattemessig-bosatt-pa-svalbard/)). A company is resident only with an **office on Svalbard and management from there** (§2-3).

A nonresident's local work/business income is liable after **at least 30 continuous days** of stay for an individual (§2-2), or business activity for a company (§2-4). Company activity and employee presence are separate tests. Property and installations on Svalbard are independently taxable under §2-2(3), including property income and qualifying property wealth, without a 30-day condition.

## The base

**Newcomers:** no Norwegian residence, including Svalbard, in the preceding ten years restricts liability to the §2-2 scope for the first five years of Svalbard residence. This is a limited-liability regime, not a general tax holiday. The office can set it aside for an income year where a significant amount, defined by the Act as **12 G**, is excluded (§2-1(2)). Keep the prior residence history and all excluded income/wealth. Do not hardcode the payroll G timing into this separate discretionary provision without an official determination.

**Residents:** worldwide income/wealth is the starting point for individuals and worldwide income for companies, subject to the Act's exceptions. For employment or business **performed outside Svalbard**, show that it is taxable under other Norwegian tax or in a foreign state before applying §§2-1/2-3 exclusions. Property/installations outside Svalbard and their income have a separate location-based exclusion. A foreign payer alone proves neither foreign work nor taxable foreign source. Allocate related costs, interest and debt consistently under Act §3-2 and Income Tax Act §§4-31, 6-91; do not deduct costs of exempt income against taxable Svalbard profit by default.

**Departure:** resident liability ends from the income year in which outside days exceed **183 in a rolling twelve-month period**. Whole or part outside days count and absences need not be continuous. It is **more than** 183, not at least 183. Necessary medical treatment or similarly weighty reasons allow a specific office decision preserving residence after an overall assessment ([Svalbard regulation §8](https://lovdata.no/dokument/SF/forskrift/1997-12-15-1305)); it is not an automatic travel-day exclusion. When residence ceases from the start of that income year, recheck each local work stay for limited liability and reconsider that year's pension/capital-income allocation. Remaining local property stays within source liability. Unconditionally earned unpaid salary is brought into charge at departure under [Act §3-1 / Income Tax Act §14-3(4)(b)](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/KAPITTEL_14).

**Distributions:** for a Svalbard company paying a shareholder resident abroad within Income Tax Act §10-13, apply the enacted **20%** withholding rate. Establish recipient status and any domestic-law exemption first, including the actual §2-38 conditions for qualifying corporate recipients. Do not extend foreign-shareholder withholding mechanically to every mainland recipient: [Income Tax Act §2-35(2)(a)](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/KAPITTEL_2) expressly preserves exceptions for §§2-38 and 10-13. The personal shareholder shielding deduction is excluded for Svalbard assessment by Act §3-2. The examples assume no exemption and a recipient abroad, rather than assuming every recipient has the same result.

## Deadlines and filing

A resident with ordinary assessed income/wealth uses the annual return, ordinarily **30 April after the income year**; companies/sole traders use **31 May**, with the applicable non-working-day/extension rules ([Administration regulation §§8-2-3, 8-2-5](https://lovdata.no/dokument/SF/forskrift/2016-11-23-1360/KAPITTEL_7)). Business allocation requires the business specification and supporting attachment ([business guidance](https://www.skatteetaten.no/person/skatt/hjelp-til-riktig-skatt/svalbard/naring-pa-svalbard/)). Withholding-only temporary work is administered through the employer; assessed property/business income is a separate return obligation. Notify Skatteetaten of residence/source corrections and establish identity/reporting access.

For foreign-shareholder dividends, report the withholding under Administration Act §8-8(3) within **seven days after withholding**, or **one month for VPS-registered companies** ([regulation §8-8-3](https://lovdata.no/dokument/SF/forskrift/2016-11-23-1360/KAPITTEL_7)). Payment is due on the same day as that reporting deadline ([Payment Act §10-13](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2005-06-17-67)). This is separate from next-working-day payroll remittance. Retain the resolution, gross dividend, recipient evidence, withholding and payment proof.

Annual advance and residual payments follow Payment Act §§10-20–10-21, detailed in the individual/company guides. On cessation submit outstanding reports, final results and tax-position schedules; cessation does not cancel payment obligations. Under Income Tax Act §10-70(11), mainland exit-tax changes must be disclosed without undue delay; deferred exit-tax obligations need continuing reporting.

## Penalties and interest

Under [Svalbard Act §4-1](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1996-11-29-68), the Tax Administration Act applies. Missing mandatory information can trigger a daily coercive fine, ordinarily **0.5 court-fee units per day**, capped at **50 units** for the ordinary information failure ([Administration Act §14-1](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2016-05-27-14); [regulation §14-1-1](https://lovdata.no/dokument/SF/forskrift/2016-11-23-1360/KAPITTEL_13)). The authority's notice sets the start date; this is not an automatic charge on every late return.

Incorrect or incomplete information producing a tax advantage attracts **20%** additional tax, reduced to **10%** for the specified third-party-reported information. Intentional or grossly negligent failures can attract a further **20% or 40%**. Excusable circumstances, qualifying voluntary correction and other statutory exceptions must be tested ([Administration Act §§14-3 to 14-6](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2016-05-27-14)). These percentages apply to the tax advantage, not turnover.

Unpaid tax bears interest from the due date under [Payment Act §§11-1 and 11-6](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2005-06-17-67). The [authority's 2026 interest table](https://www.skatteetaten.no/satser/forsinkelsesrenter/) gives **12% a year for 1 January–30 June 2026** and **12.25% for 1 July–31 December 2026**. Split arrears at the change date. A payment falling due in 2027 needs the rate then in force; do not extrapolate 2026 rates.

## Cross-border points

**Mainland interaction:** [Income Tax Act §2-35(2)(c)](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/KAPITTEL_2) removes mainland tax on income/wealth **taxed to Svalbard** for mainland residents/companies. Keep proof of the Svalbard assessment. Moving does not itself prove mainland fiscal emigration; §2-1(3)'s separate residence/departure conditions still matter. The 2026 [authority S-23-2.2](https://oppslag.rettskilder.skatteetaten.no/rettskilder2/type/handboker/skatte-abc/gjeldende/skatteabc-S-23) explains the ongoing mainland residence link and why the rules must be tested separately.

**Treaties and foreign tax:** Norway's income-tax treaties do **not** apply to Svalbard ([current authority S-23-1.5 and 9.2.3](https://oppslag.rettskilder.skatteetaten.no/rettskilder2/type/handboker/skatte-abc/gjeldende/skatteabc-S-23)). The Nordic insurance convention is separate. Use the statutory Svalbard source exclusions first. Act §3-2 imports Income Tax Act rules with express exclusions, and §§16-20–16-29 contain domestic foreign-tax credit rules. The exact adapted operation for Svalbard foreign capital income/wealth, including allocation and excess credits, has not been verified here; do not promise automatic credit for a foreign levy. This ordinary relief question remains partial.

**Mainland move to Svalbard:** Income Tax Act §2-35(2)(b) expressly extends §10-70 to a person becoming Svalbard-resident and to specified transfers to Svalbard recipients. Svalbard is treated as EEA for this specific rule. For a 2026 departure, §10-70 covers the listed share/account/fund/derivative assets, deems disposal just before the change, applies the **NOK 3m** departure gain allowance and a distinct **NOK 100,000** annual transfer threshold. Gain uses market value less the applicable tax basis, with statutory loss/immigration-basis rules. A qualifying deferral runs up to **12 years**, with security rules and annual-instalment or interest-bearing end-payment choices (§10-70(7)). Disposals, transfers and distributions can accelerate payment; **70% of distributions** is the statutory amount in §10-70(8), subject to its safeguards. A move to Svalbard is not a tax-free alternative to mainland exit tax. Full account-specific valuation and deferral administration are outside this guide's verified computation.

**Leaving Svalbard/business cessation:** check unpaid remuneration, final gains/losses and the continuing local-property source rule. Imported Income Tax Act §§9-14 and 10-71 address assets leaving the tax base and company emigration. Their Svalbard adaptations, exclusions and payment computations are not established here, so no automatic tax-free exit or invented flat exit levy is stated. This remains a core XB gap in the README.

## Two worked examples that recompute

**Exactly 30 days qualifies.** A nonresident adult has exactly 30 continuous countable local days, excluding travel days, and NOK 90,000 covered salary below 12 G. Income tax is NOK 7,200. Membership must be established separately; insurance is excluded from this example. A foreign tax-treaty work threshold is not substituted.

```oa-check
name: Svalbard nonresident exactly thirty days 2026
inputs:
  local_salary: 90000
steps:
  income_tax: local_salary*0.08
  after_income_tax: local_salary-income_tax
expected:
  income_tax: 7200
  after_income_tax: 82800
```

**Foreign shareholder distribution.** A company resolves a gross NOK 500,000 dividend to an individual resident abroad. Assume the §10-13 liability applies and no domestic exemption. Withholding is NOK 100,000 and net payment NOK 400,000. This does not determine the recipient's home-country tax or credit.

```oa-check
name: Svalbard foreign individual dividend withholding 2026
inputs:
  gross_dividend: 500000
steps:
  withholding: gross_dividend*0.20
  net: gross_dividend-withholding
expected:
  withholding: 100000
  net: 400000
```

## Ask the client first

- What are the exact arrival, departure and rolling absence dates, plus ten-year Norwegian residence history?
- Where are actual work, business, management and property, and which assessments prove taxability elsewhere?
- What foreign income/wealth, recipient identities, shareholdings and proposed distributions exist?
- What assets, tax bases and deferred mainland exit obligations exist on arrival/departure?
- Are there medical-absence decisions, foreign levies, prior returns or unresolved office determinations?
