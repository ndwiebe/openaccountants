# Grenada: ordinary property-transfer tax

## Scope and source limits

This Guide covers ordinary land transfers under the Property Transfer Tax Act, Chapter 257C, using Parliament's published consolidation and the [IRD property-transfer summary](https://ird.gd/taxes/property-transfer-tax). The consolidation lists amendments through 2010; it is not represented as a complete 2026 consolidation. A later 2016 investment-relief amendment was retrieved separately. Full amendment and commencement reconciliation, current forms and independent review remain outstanding.

The ordinary rates and thresholds below agree with the current official indexed IRD summary. This Guide does not certify eligibility for investment or tourism concessions, treaty or regional-nationality treatment, or complete closing costs.

## Classify the transaction and each party

Section 2 includes transfers by sale, exchange, gift or other lifetime disposition. Property includes land interests, leases of **ten years or more**, and company shares, including debentures. A transaction need not be an outright sale of freehold land to enter the Act. This Guide's calculations concern ordinary land transfers, not shares or long-lease valuation. [Act, section 2](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap257C-PROPERTY-TRANSFER-TAX-ACT.pdf#page=2).

“Citizen” has a statutory extended definition. It includes specified dependants, qualifying partnerships and companies controlled by citizens. Section 2(2) recognises control where citizens hold more than half the shares or sufficient voting power to determine the company's affairs. For a company without share capital, section 2(3) uses effective management in Grenada and more than half citizen directors. Incorporation in Grenada alone does not establish the classification. Obtain ownership, voting and management evidence for the actual parties. [Act, section 2](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap257C-PROPERTY-TRANSFER-TAX-ACT.pdf#page=2).

## Ordinary rates and taxable value

Section 5(1) provides the following separate liabilities for land:

| Party | Ordinary rate |
|---|---:|
| Citizen vendor | 5% |
| Noncitizen vendor | 15% |
| Noncitizen purchaser | 10% |

The vendor's and purchaser's taxes are separate. Section 5(1) does not impose the ordinary purchaser charge on a citizen purchaser. This does not exempt that person from other acquisition charges. [Act, section 5](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap257C-PROPERTY-TRANSFER-TAX-ACT.pdf#page=4).

For nongift land transfers, section 5(3) taxes only the value **exceeding EC$20,000**. Use the established value and calculate `max(value − 20,000, 0)` before applying the relevant ordinary rate. The threshold is not a deduction from the resulting tax bill. [Act, section 5(3)](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap257C-PROPERTY-TRANSFER-TAX-ACT.pdf#page=4).

The Act expresses the rates by reference to market value. Under section 8, the Comptroller obtains a valuation where there is no money consideration or the consideration does not represent fair market value. A nominal related-party sale price does not establish the taxable value. Keep price, valuation and any determination distinct; do not compute tax merely on profit over the seller's original cost. [Act, sections 5 and 8](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap257C-PROPERTY-TRANSFER-TAX-ACT.pdf#page=4).

**Gift boundary:** section 5(2) applies the **EC$150,000** gift threshold and expressly references the vendor-rate paragraphs (a), (b) and (c). Section 6(1)(e) exempts gifts not exceeding that value. This Guide does not assume that the larger gift threshold automatically determines every noncitizen recipient's liability above that amount; obtain the transaction-specific treatment. [Act, sections 5(2) and 6](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap257C-PROPERTY-TRANSFER-TAX-ACT.pdf#page=4).

## Mechanical examples

Assume an ordinary nongift land transfer, value EC$500,000 established for tax purposes, no exemption and no special development/investment relief. Taxable value is EC$480,000.

- Citizen seller: 480,000 × 5% = **EC$24,000**.
- Noncitizen seller: 480,000 × 15% = **EC$72,000**.
- Noncitizen buyer: 480,000 × 10% = **EC$48,000**, in addition to the applicable seller liability.

Thus a citizen seller/noncitizen buyer transaction has EC$72,000 combined transfer tax, while a noncitizen seller/noncitizen buyer transaction has EC$120,000. These totals do not include stamp duty, legal fees or other acquisition/holding obligations. At a value of exactly EC$20,000 the ordinary nongift land base under section 5(3) is zero. [Act, section 5](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap257C-PROPERTY-TRANSFER-TAX-ACT.pdf#page=4).

## Exemptions, special relief and completion evidence

Section 6 includes specified mortgage-related instruments, transfers by operation of law to a liquidator or bankruptcy trustee, specified trust transfers, testamentary/intestacy transfers, Crown/publicly funded statutory-board transfers and transfers by the named charitable/nonprofit bodies. Match the exact provision and conditions. In particular, the mortgage exemption has a declaration condition in section 6(2); a later sale by a mortgagee is not automatically an exempt mortgage instrument. [Act, sections 5(1a) and 6](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap257C-PROPERTY-TRANSFER-TAX-ACT.pdf#page=4).

Sections 5A–5B create a separate regime for qualifying villas/apartments in approved tourism developments, with Cabinet approval by Order. A tourist location or marketing description is not proof of that approval. Obtain the Order and transfer history before computing a concession. [Act, sections 5A–5B](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap257C-PROPERTY-TRANSFER-TAX-ACT.pdf#page=4).

[Act 9 of 2016](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Act-No.-9-of-2016-Property-Transfer-Tax-Amendment-Act.pdf) separately changes qualifying-investment waivers and contains commencement and clawback provisions. This Guide does not operationalise those waivers: the complete intervening amendments, commencement order, qualifying investment instruments and approval evidence have not been verified together.

Sections 7 and 18 require evidence of payment or no-tax treatment for recording the transfer. Obtain the Comptroller's stamp/receipt and the required evidence before treating the conveyance as ready for recording. Do not invent a universal payment deadline from a deadline that concerns reassessment or appeal. Stamp duty and landholding permissions are separate questions. [Act, sections 7 and 18](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap257C-PROPERTY-TRANSFER-TAX-ACT.pdf#page=6).
