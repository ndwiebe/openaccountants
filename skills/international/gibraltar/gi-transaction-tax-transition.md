# Gibraltar goods transaction-tax transition v0.2

> **Source-cited draft.** Professional review is pending. Figures are limited to the periods and assumptions below. This Guide prepares working papers; it is not authority to file or pay.

## Scope

Prepare an evidence-led review of goods imports, local production and the 2026 transaction-tax transition. This is a transition research draft, not a customs declaration or an exhaustive commodity-rate catalogue.

Sources were accessed on 22 October 2026. A dated rate table controls its stated period; an undated guidance page does not prove that all later amendments have been incorporated.

## Enacted scope and commencement

Section 98 charges goods **produced in Gibraltar or imported into Gibraltar**, subject to exemptions and exclusions. The consolidation's commencement table records the remaining provisions commencing on 15 July 2026 under LN 2026/148. Use the enacted goods charge rather than the earlier March press explanation as the scope authority. This Guide does not establish a general retail VAT or a service-tax regime. [Act, commencement table and section 98](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/treaty-on-gibraltar/2026-11/2026-11(15-07-26).pdf).

Under section 100, the declarant or person on whose behalf entry is declared is liable. Others involved in irregular entry are also liable; for Gibraltar production, the producer is liable. Identify the liable person before allocating the charge to a customer or an intermediary. [Section 100](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/treaty-on-gibraltar/2026-11/2026-11(15-07-26).pdf).

## Identify the chargeable event

Schedule 2 paragraph 1 charges and collects tax at these events:

- Goods in Union free circulation transported under the special Gibraltar transit procedure: presentation to customs.
- Goods from outside the customs union transported under the relevant special transit procedure: presentation to customs.
- Within the special fiscal/customs procedures: Union goods put on the Gibraltar market, or a customs debt arising for non-Union goods.
- Gibraltar-produced goods leaving the production site.
- Discovery of irregular entry.
- Travellers' non-commercial goods above the applicable quantitative thresholds: declaration or completion of checks.

For local production, leaving includes physical dispatch regardless of destination or purpose **and transfer of the right to dispose as owner even if the goods remain at the facility**. Record both dates; do not wait for physical delivery where ownership-disposal rights have already transferred. The treaty Annex 23 traveller thresholds and Annexes 19/21 procedures need separate verification for the actual movement. [Schedule 2, paragraph 1, printed pages 103–104](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/treaty-on-gibraltar/2026-11/2026-11(15-07-26).pdf).

## Calculate the taxable amount

For imports, begin with customs value. Add import taxes, duties, levies and other charges, including excise but excluding transaction tax itself, and incidental costs such as commission, packing, transport and insurance, **only insofar as not already included**. Reconcile invoices and the customs valuation to prevent duplicate freight or insurance. [Schedule 2, paragraph 2(1)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/treaty-on-gibraltar/2026-11/2026-11(15-07-26).pdf).

For Gibraltar production, use open-market value when the goods leave production: the amount payable at the same marketing stage in fair competition to an arm's-length Gibraltar supplier. If no comparable supply can be identified, the statutory floor is purchase price of those or similar goods, or, absent a purchase price, cost price at that time. A cost floor is not an unconditional permission to ignore an identifiable comparable market value. [Paragraph 2(2)–(4)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/treaty-on-gibraltar/2026-11/2026-11(15-07-26).pdf).

## Rate selection and period controls

The enacted standard-rate sequence is 15% for year 1, 16% for year 2, and after 24 months 17% or the lowest Member State rate at that time. **The statutory periods refer to entry into force of the TGEU.** This Guide has not reconciled that trigger with provisional application and does not assign automatic July 2027/2028 rate-change dates. Section 99 permits rate/category amendments, subject to its conditions and exception. Check the current schedule and implementing measures on the transaction date. [Section 99 and Schedule 2, paragraph 3(1)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/treaty-on-gibraltar/2026-11/2026-11(15-07-26).pdf).

The schedule's 5% categories include agricultural inputs subject to capital-goods exclusions and the chemical pesticide/fertiliser time limit; live equines; specified plants/floricultural goods; children's clothing, footwear and car seats; bicycles including electric bicycles; defined art, collectors' items and antiques; and qualifying rescue/first-aid equipment supplied to public or eligible nonprofit bodies. Classification depends on the full statutory description, recipient and any incorporated EU definition. [Paragraph 3(2), printed page 105](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/treaty-on-gibraltar/2026-11/2026-11(15-07-26).pdf).

The 0% categories cover specified foodstuffs excluding alcoholic beverages, water, qualifying pharmaceutical/hygiene products, medical/disability equipment, qualifying publications, and solar panels for the specified buildings. Publication exclusions include predominantly advertising, video or audible-music content. These are category summaries, not a commodity classification decision; retain the exact applicable row and evidence. A zero rate does not by itself remove declaration obligations. [Paragraph 3(3), printed pages 105–106](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/treaty-on-gibraltar/2026-11/2026-11(15-07-26).pdf).

## Recomputable examples

These are assumed classification/rate cases, not rulings on particular goods:

1. **Import:** customs value £10,000 already includes £400 freight. Separately chargeable excise £1,000 and packing £200 are not included. Taxable amount = £10,000 + £1,000 + £200 = **£11,200**. At an established 15% rate, tax = **£1,680**. Adding the £400 again would overstate tax by £60.
2. **Local production:** established comparable open-market value £8,000; production cost £6,000. At an established 15% rate, tax = **£1,200**, not £900. If disposal rights transfer before dispatch, leaving occurs on that transfer under paragraph 1(2)(b).
3. **No comparable supply:** no purchase price is available and evidenced cost price is £6,000. That establishes a floor of £6,000, not proof that any lower invoice price is acceptable. At 15%, tax on the floor is £900; confirm the final taxable valuation before using that amount.
4. **Rate sensitivity:** an independently established £1,000 taxable amount gives £150 at 15%, £50 at 5%, or £0 at 0%. The arithmetic does not prove eligibility for a reduced category.

## Exemptions and export refunds

Section 101 applies mandatory EU VAT exemptions by asking whether the goods would qualify if imported, supplied or acquired in the EU. A discretionary exemption requires the Committee approval and Minister's Gazette notice described there. It expressly exempts LNG imported and used for electricity production and electricity produced in Gibraltar. Do not treat all fuels or all energy equipment as exempt. [Section 101](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/treaty-on-gibraltar/2026-11/2026-11(15-07-26).pdf).

For commercial exports to the EU, section 102 requires proof satisfactory to the Collector that EU VAT was paid. For exports outside the customs union, it requires export through a designated customs post and notification to the Collector by that post. Goods consumed in Gibraltar do not qualify for refund. Obtain export and destination-tax evidence; this is not an automatic credit for every overseas sale. Refund forms, submission deadlines and operational processing remain unverified. [Section 102](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/treaty-on-gibraltar/2026-11/2026-11(15-07-26).pdf).

## Working-paper procedure

Record the goods, liable person, date/event, customs procedure and supporting documents. Establish exemption or rate classification; reconcile the valuation components; compute tax; match the declaration and payment evidence. Keep any export-refund claim and income-tax transition relief separate. Review bonded stock and other transitional procedures before using these rules for pre-commencement stock.

## Transitional business relief

The September announcement describes relief for qualifying certified retail/wholesale businesses for the first 90 days from 15 July, ending 12 October 2026: the excess of transaction tax over the counterfactual import duty, capped at 50% of preceding-period tax. It specifies exclusions, compliance conditions, OFT certification, payment evidence, state-aid limits and incompatibility with another support scheme. An extension requires its own evidence. [Government announcement](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/press-releases/minister-arias-vasquez-publishes-transaction-tax-deductions-rules-2026-6922026-12358); [Transaction Tax (Deductions) Rules 2026](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/legislations/transaction-tax-deductions-rules-2026-8890).

**Outstanding verification:** reconcile the deductions rules themselves and any extension after 12 October; treaty entry-into-force versus provisional-application rate triggers; incorporated commodity definitions, customs procedures and traveller thresholds; transitional stock; declaration/payment deadlines, refund procedure and subsequent amendments.
