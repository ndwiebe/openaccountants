# Gibraltar: company accounts, audit and annual reporting

## Scope

This Guide addresses ordinary private mainstream companies limited by shares. Section 237 distinguishes mainstream companies from non-profit companies, licensed banks and insurance companies. Public-interest entities are generally excluded from Part VII simplifications by section 237A. Group accounts, regulated entities and non-profit reporting require separate analysis; do not apply the ordinary-company workflow without checking that scope. [Companies Act, sections 237–237A](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/companies/2014-19/2014-19(01-08-25).pdf).

## Keep books and select the accounting basis

Section 239 requires proper books for **five years**, covering receipts and spending, goods and services sold or purchased, and assets and liabilities. Keep them at the registered office or another place selected by the directors, accessible for their inspection. This company-law period does not settle longer retention duties under tax or other laws. [Companies Act, section 239](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/companies/2014-19/2014-19(01-08-25).pdf).

Mainstream companies prepare either section 243 Non-IAS accounts or accounts under the **UK-adopted international accounting standards** defined in section 237. After choosing IAS accounts, section 238 requires that basis to continue unless a specified change of circumstances permits departure. Do not describe the choice as an unrestricted annual election or assume every reference to international standards means any version of IFRS. [Companies Act, sections 237–238](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/companies/2014-19/2014-19(01-08-25).pdf).

Prepare the first profit-and-loss account by a date no later than 18 months after incorporation and subsequently for each financial year, with the corresponding balance sheet. Section 240 also governs presentation to the general meeting. A reduced public filing does not remove the underlying accounting obligation. [Companies Act, section 240](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/companies/2014-19/2014-19(01-08-25).pdf).

## Establish size before claiming a filing concession

Schedule 9 uses at least **two of three** conditions, subject to its exclusions and year-to-year rules:

| Classification | Net turnover no greater than | Balance-sheet total no greater than | Average employees no greater than |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Micro | £632,000 | £316,000 | 10 |
| Small | £10.2 million | £5.1 million | 50 |
| Medium, if not micro or small | £36 million | £18 million | 250 |

These are company-size tests. Net turnover is ordinary goods/services income after trade discounts and sales-based taxes; balance-sheet total is assets, not net equity. Public companies do not qualify in these three categories. For an established company, crossing more than one condition changes classification only after two consecutive years; the first financial year uses that year's conditions. Schedule 9 also proportionately adjusts the specified small/medium turnover limits for a period other than a year. [Companies Act, Schedule 9, paragraphs 1ZA–6](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/companies/2014-19/2014-19(01-08-25).pdf).

Example: in its first twelve-month year, an ordinary company with turnover £9 million, assets £5 million and 60 average employees satisfies two small-company limits. It can qualify as small despite exceeding the employee limit, subject to the scope exclusions. It has **not** thereby established an audit exemption.

Under section 254(6), a small mainstream company can elect to deliver only its balance sheet. The abridged formats and associated disclosures still need to comply with the applicable schedules. Record the classification calculation, prior-year position and excluded-status checks before choosing a reduced filing. [Companies Act, section 254 and Schedules 9 and 12](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/companies/2014-19/2014-19(01-08-25).pdf).

## Audit: company size and tax-return evidence differ

Section 255 starts with auditor appointment, subject to section 259. Section 259 requires a small mainstream company and either the specified absence of an income-return obligation or its linked Income Tax Act condition. Section 255(2) also permits a special resolution requiring an audit for the period even where the exemption otherwise applies. A small-company balance-sheet filing alone proves neither of these audit conditions. [Companies Act, sections 255 and 259](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/companies/2014-19/2014-19(01-08-25).pdf).

There is a material cross-reference problem: section 259(1)(b) names Income Tax Act section **30(1)(c)** and describes an unaudited-return exemption. In the current Income Tax Act, paragraph (c) concerns the application to branches, while the substantive lower-income rule is in **(bb)**. This Guide does not silently rewrite the statute or certify a company-law audit waiver. Obtain a legal determination of the exemption before relying on it.

For the **tax return**, the current section 30(1)(b) requires audited accounts at assessable income **at least £1,750,000** for a twelve-month period. Paragraph (bb) requires accounts with an independent accountant's report below that boundary. Shorter periods use the appropriate monthly proportion. This measure is assessable income, not the Schedule 9 net-turnover measure. [Income Tax Act, section 30(1)(b)–(cb)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/income-tax/2010-21/2010-21(23-03-26).pdf).

Thus exactly £1,750,000 is on the audited side, and £1,749,999 is below it for a twelve-month period. For six months, **£1,750,000 × 6/12 = £875,000**, with equality again on the audited side. These examples address the tax-return attachment rule; they do not resolve the company-law cross-reference.

An independent accountant must meet section 30(1)(cb)'s approval, independence and practising-certificate conditions. Below-threshold accounts are signed by two directors or the sole director. Do not assume that any bookkeeper or accountant can issue the required report. [Income Tax Act, section 30(1)(ca)–(cb)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/income-tax/2010-21/2010-21(23-03-26).pdf).

## Accounts filing and annual return are separate

For the private companies in scope, section 272 allows **12 months after the financial year-end** for accounts and reports. The first-year rule also specifies 12 months after that year-end. Section 254 requires the filed accounts to be signed by two directors or, if there is only one, that director. Subject to the applicable concessions, deliver the accounts and required directors' and auditors' reports. [Companies Act, sections 254 and 272](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/companies/2014-19/2014-19(01-08-25).pdf).

For a 31 December 2025 year-end, the ordinary private-company accounts deadline is **31 December 2026**. The supplementary-fee schedule's later 13-month boundary is not a filing extension.

The annual return under section 188 has its own return date, normally the incorporation anniversary or anniversary of the different date used for the last return. It is signed by a director or secretary and ordinarily delivered within **30 days** of its made-up date. Section 189 private investment schemes have a distinct rule outside this Guide. The annual return does not replace accounts or the corporate tax return. [Companies Act, sections 188–189](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/companies/2014-19/2014-19(01-08-25).pdf); [corporate tax Guide](gi-corporate-tax.md).

## Filing fees and lateness

The published accounts filing fee is **£26**. Section 426 adds £180.50 when accounts are filed more than 13 but no more than 24 months after the relevant period, and £309 after 24 months. Therefore the published totals are **£26 + £180.50 = £206.50** and **£26 + £309 = £335**. These charges do not exhaust the offence and penalty provisions in section 269. An account filed after its 12-month deadline is late even before the supplementary-fee threshold is reached. [Companies Act, sections 269 and 426](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/companies/2014-19/2014-19(01-08-25).pdf); [Registry fee table](https://www.companieshouse.gi/publications/C0019.pdf).

## Working paper and review

Retain reconciled books, the selected accounting basis, period dates, size-test calculation, audit analysis, board approval, signed accounts, filed copies and receipts. Keep separate calendar entries for accounts, annual return, tax and officer changes. See the [formation Guide](gi-company-formation.md) for initial officer and office filings.
