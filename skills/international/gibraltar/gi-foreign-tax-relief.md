# Gibraltar: domestic relief for foreign income tax

## Scope and eligibility

This Guide addresses **section 37 domestic relief**, not entitlement under a tax treaty. Begin with the actual Gibraltar charge, foreign charge and the profits or gains to which each relates. Section 37(1) requires proof satisfactory to the Commissioner that the person has paid, by deduction or otherwise, or is liable to pay tax in both jurisdictions on the **same profits or gains**. It is not enough that a group company paid tax elsewhere on unrelated income. [Income Tax Act, section 37(1)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/income-tax/2010-21/2010-21(23-03-26).pdf).

The basic amount is the lesser of Gibraltar tax attributable to those profits or gains and foreign income tax on that income, subject to the further limitations below. Foreign “income tax” includes a tax calculated on a similar basis or of a similar nature to Gibraltar income tax. Do not treat a foreign payment as creditable merely because its name includes “tax”; establish its legal character. [Section 37(1) and (7)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/income-tax/2010-21/2010-21(23-03-26).pdf).

## Source restriction and residence-only exclusion

Section 37(8) limits the provision to tax paid in the country in or from which the revenue generated by the underlying business activity arose. Identify that activity and revenue source with contracts, invoices and operational evidence; the payer's address alone is not a completed source analysis. [Section 37(8)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/income-tax/2010-21/2010-21(23-03-26).pdf).

Section 37(8A) excludes Gibraltar tax on Gibraltar-source income where the provision would otherwise apply only because the taxpayer's foreign country of tax residence charges that income on the basis of residence. Do not use a blanket “taxed twice, therefore Gibraltar grants the credit” rule for that situation. Relief in the other country or under an applicable agreement requires its own analysis. [Section 37(8A)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/income-tax/2010-21/2010-21(23-03-26).pdf).

Section 37(1) refers to tax paid or payable while subsection (8) uses tax paid. If a proposed claim concerns assessed but unpaid foreign tax, reconcile those provisions and the evidence required with the Commissioner before relying on the credit. This Guide does not resolve that interaction by deleting either qualification.

## Allocate the Gibraltar tax before applying the cap

Section 37(4) attributes Gibraltar tax proportionately:

**Gibraltar tax on the relevant income = total Gibraltar tax before section 37 relief × relevant charged profits or gains ÷ total charged profits or gains.**

Use the amounts actually charged for the relevant assessment year or accounting period. Do not substitute worldwide revenue, accounting profit or a headline tax rate without reconciling them to the statutory numerator and denominator. A nil denominator or disputed allocation needs separate analysis. [Section 37(4)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/income-tax/2010-21/2010-21(23-03-26).pdf).

Example: total charged profits are **£100,000**, of which **£40,000** are the relevant income. Total Gibraltar tax before section 37 relief is assumed to be **£15,000**. The allocated tax is **£15,000 × £40,000 / £100,000 = £6,000**. If qualifying foreign tax on that same income is £8,000, the initial relief is **min(£6,000, £8,000) = £6,000**. If it is £4,500, the initial relief is £4,500. These are computational examples assuming all source and eligibility conditions are met, not a determination of the tax rate or income source.

## Account for relief allowed abroad

Section 37(2) reduces Gibraltar relief by the amount of relevant unilateral relief allowable abroad for Gibraltar tax on those profits or gains. Section 37(3) calculates the foreign tax before that foreign unilateral relief and permits a certificate from the corresponding foreign tax authority as evidence of the tax or relief amount. Do not deduct the foreign relief twice or ignore it because it has not yet been claimed. [Section 37(2)–(3)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/income-tax/2010-21/2010-21(23-03-26).pdf).

Continuing the £6,000 initial-relief example, if the relevant foreign unilateral relief allowable is **£2,000**, the reduction leaves **£4,000** of Gibraltar relief under the stated arithmetic. The actual foreign entitlement must be evidenced under that country's law. This example does not address a case where the foreign reduction exceeds the initial Gibraltar relief.

## Claim and adjustment deadlines

An ordinary section 37 claim must be made no later than **six years after the end of the assessment year or accounting period** concerned. For an accounting period ending 31 December 2025, that gives **31 December 2031**. This claim limit does not defer the ordinary return or payment deadline. [Section 37(5)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/income-tax/2010-21/2010-21(23-03-26).pdf); [corporate tax Guide](gi-corporate-tax.md).

A later adjustment to Gibraltar tax, foreign tax or relevant foreign relief can make the original credit excessive or insufficient. Section 37(6) provides a special assessment/claim route within six years from the time all material assessments, adjustments and determinations have been made. Record those events; do not assume the ordinary deadline alone closes the case or that every foreign payment restarts it. [Section 37(6)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/income-tax/2010-21/2010-21(23-03-26).pdf).

## Claim working paper

Prepare and retain:

1. Identification of the person, both tax periods and the same income taxed in each jurisdiction.
2. The Gibraltar computation before relief and the section 37(4) allocation.
3. The foreign assessment, withholding certificate or payment evidence, with tax type and legal basis.
4. Evidence of the underlying business activity and revenue source, with the subsection (8A) exclusion considered explicitly.
5. The foreign unilateral-relief analysis, any authority certificate, and the resulting Gibraltar adjustment.
6. The claim date, submission evidence and a log of later foreign or Gibraltar revisions.

These are suggested evidence controls to support the statutory test, not an assertion that a particular claim form has been verified. Preserve records under the [bookkeeping and tax-records Guide](gi-bookkeeping-and-tax-records.md).
