# Gibraltar: formation of a private company limited by shares

## Scope and legal basis

This Guide addresses ordinary private companies limited by shares. It does not complete the formation route for public companies, guarantee companies, protected cells, overseas branches or re-domiciliations. The official legislation index identified the Companies Act consolidation dated **1 August 2025** as its current version when checked on 22 October 2026. Statutory references below are to that text. [Act and version history](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/legislations/companies-act-2014-3106).

## Subscribers and constitution

One or more persons can form a company for a lawful purpose. Each subscriber agrees to become a member and, for a company with share capital, to take at least one share. The memorandum states the company name, Gibraltar registered-office jurisdiction, limited liability and required capital particulars. Each subscriber signs before at least one attesting witness. For the ordinary private-company route, the articles restrict share transfers and prohibit public invitations to subscribe for shares or debentures. [Companies Act, sections 5–7 and 19](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/companies/2014-19/2014-19(01-08-25).pdf).

Obtain name acceptance before submitting the incorporation documents. Using a licensed provider is recommended by the government but is not compulsory for a founder incorporating directly; providing registration or management services **by way of business** requires licensing. Do not transplant Guernsey's CSP-only applicant rule into Gibraltar. [Government incorporation guidance](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/business/companies-house/companies).

## Assemble the application

Section 9 requires the memorandum, registration application, prescribed supporting information and statement of compliance. Identify the proposed name, limited-by-shares status, private-company status and intended office. If an agent delivers the application, state that agent's name and address. Include proposed articles to the extent not supplied by the statutory default model-articles provisions. [Companies Act, section 9](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/companies/2014-19/2014-19(01-08-25).pdf).

The current FINC01 form provides choices for adopting model articles unchanged, adopting them with attached amendments, or supplying bespoke articles. It also requests the company contact email and principal-activity SIC code. Use the actual form and relevant continuation pages rather than rewriting its fields. [Application FINC01, pages 1–4](https://www.companieshouse.gi/publications/FINC01.pdf).

For a share company, section 10 requires the total shares taken on formation, their aggregate nominal value, class totals and rights, paid/unpaid amounts and subscriber allocations. FINC01 separately labels authorised-capital tables and initial-shareholding details: reconcile both instead of assuming every authorised share has been subscribed. [Companies Act, section 10](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/companies/2014-19/2014-19(01-08-25).pdf); [FINC01, sections G1–G5](https://www.companieshouse.gi/publications/FINC01.pdf).

Example: if a proposed structure authorises 1,000 ordinary £1 shares and two subscribers each take 100 fully paid shares, the authorised nominal amount is **1,000 × £1 = £1,000**, while the initial subscriptions total **(100 + 100) × £1 = £200**, with £0 unpaid on those subscribed shares. This is illustrative arithmetic, not a prescribed minimum capital or proof that the remaining 800 shares have been issued. Confirm the chosen structure and rights against the constitution before filing.

## Office, director and secretary

A Gibraltar registered office is required. Companies House says the first office notice accompanies incorporation documents. Providing registered-office services to the public is a regulated activity. Subsequent office changes must be notified within **30 days**; waiting for an annual return only satisfies that duty if that return is due and made within the same period. The guidance lists a £26 filing charge and an additional £26 for late notification. [Registered-office guidance, pages 1–2](https://www.companieshouse.gi/publications/C0002.pdf).

A private company needs at least **one director** and every company needs a secretary. A sole director cannot also be that company's secretary. Section 216(4) also prohibits the specified corporate-secretary/sole-director arrangements that would replicate the same conflict through a corporation. Directors must take reasonable steps to appoint a secretary with suitable knowledge and experience. [Companies Act, sections 215–217](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/companies/2014-19/2014-19(01-08-25).pdf).

Where the secretary is appointed by the articles, obtain signed consent and deliver it for registration before the articles are registered. FINC02 is the Registry's consent form, identifying the proposed company and secretary, address, signature and date. [Companies Act, section 216(5)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/companies/2014-19/2014-19(01-08-25).pdf); [FINC02](https://www.companieshouse.gi/publications/FINC02.pdf).

Companies House instructs private companies to file their first particulars of directors and secretaries within **14 days of first appointment**. Use separate forms for appointments outside the same 14-day window. The first form is signed by a first director or secretary, identifying the signatory and capacity. Subsequent changes have a distinct signature policy; an incoming officer's signature is normally not accepted for a change. The published charge is £26 per form, with a further £26 for lateness. [Officer guidance, pages 1–3](https://www.companieshouse.gi/publications/C0010.pdf).

The consolidation retains section 9(4)(c)'s reference to a section 12 statement, while section 12 itself is marked repealed. Do not invent a section 12 form or omit the separate officer filing. Reconcile any additional officer information requested at submission with the Registrar before filing. This textual discrepancy remains an explicit review point.

## Compliance declaration and fees

Section 13 permits the compliance statement to be made by a Supreme Court solicitor, a qualifying barrister acting as solicitor engaged in formation, or a person named in the articles as director or secretary. FINC23 implements the last route. A proposed director not named in the articles cannot be assumed to meet that wording solely by being proposed for appointment. [Companies Act, section 13](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/companies/2014-19/2014-19(01-08-25).pdf); [FINC23](https://www.companieshouse.gi/publications/FINC23.pdf).

The 2025 fee regulations, effective **1 August 2025**, set incorporation at **£100**, with **£10 stamp duty** payable at incorporation. These two listed charges total **£110**. This excludes professional services and separately chargeable filings, including the officer-particulars filing described above. [Legal Notice 211/2025, regulation 2 and Schedule 24 replacement](https://gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/companies/2014-19/2025=211.pdf).

The fee table lists a £200 same-day urgency fee where correct documents arrive before noon and a £500 two-hour service with correct documents lodged between 9am and 3pm. Confirm the selected service and total payable; the general business page's older “24 hours” wording is not a guaranteed turnaround. [Companies House fee table, page 1](https://www.companieshouse.gi/publications/C0019.pdf).

## Beneficial-ownership reporting after incorporation

The July 2026 consolidation requires a Gibraltar-incorporated corporate or legal entity to obtain and hold **adequate, accurate and current** beneficial-ownership information, including interests held, and submit it as the Registrar requires. If the company has a secretary, supply the secretary with a copy of the information submitted. Do not treat the register of members or incorporation application as the completed beneficial-ownership return. [UBO Regulations, regulation 6](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/proceeds-of-crime/2017s122/2017s122(15-07-26).pdf).

For an ordinary unlisted company, regulation 3 examines ultimate direct/indirect ownership or control, then control through other means after the stated investigation conditions, and finally its specified fallback. The ownership indications include a shareholding of **25% plus one share** or an ownership interest of **more than 25%**, including the specified controlled-corporate chains. These are indications, not a rule that a person below the threshold can never control the company. Listed entities, majority-owned listed subsidiaries and the statutory fallback have specific treatment; do not substitute an automatic director designation without completing the regulation 3 analysis. [Regulation 3(1)–(3)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/proceeds-of-crime/2017s122/2017s122(15-07-26).pdf).

**Boundary example:** with 100 equal shares, 25 shares is exactly 25% and does not meet the 25%-plus-one-share indication; 26 shares does. This does not clear the 25-share holder of reporting if other control facts apply. Trace voting arrangements, controlled intermediaries and actual control before identifying the reportable owner.

Regulation 6(4) permits the Registrar to specify identity particulars including names/aliases, birth details, nationality, usual residence, residential/service addresses and occupation, alongside acquisition/cessation dates and the nature, extent and holding method of the beneficial interest. A beneficial owner who is also a shareholder must report the percentage legally owned under regulation 6(5). Collect evidence securely; this Guide does not imply every field is public. [Regulation 6(4)–(6)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/proceeds-of-crime/2017s122/2017s122(15-07-26).pdf).

| Obligation | Statutory timing |
|---|---|
| New company's beneficial-ownership submission | Within 30 days of incorporation, regulation 8(3). |
| Company material change or significant inaccuracy | Within 30 days beginning with occurrence of the change or discovery of the inaccuracy, or a later time agreed with the Registrar; include the event/discovery date, regulation 8(4). |
| New UBO tells the company of their status and provides reasonably required information | Within 15 days of becoming a UBO, regulation 8A. This is separate from the company's Registrar filing. |

Source: [regulations 8–8A](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/proceeds-of-crime/2017s122/2017s122(15-07-26).pdf). Retain trigger dates, evidence supplied and submission receipts. Regulation 8(6) contains a limited-information route where a relevant trust's trustees have supplied the required information under the Trusts (Information) Regulations 2025; it is not a general exemption for trust ownership. Establish that separate regime before using the limitation.

Nominee arrangements and director appointors have **additional information duties** under regulations 6A–6B and 8B–8C. The Registrar can require those particulars; a UBO submission alone does not prove compliance. Regulations 8B(3) and 8C(3) prescribe 30 days from incorporation for their new-entity submissions, and their paragraph (4) provisions address subsequent changes/inaccuracies. The core nominee/director-to-company rules are set out below; historical transitional cases and portal-specific verification remain open. [Regulations 6A–6B, 8B–8C](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/proceeds-of-crime/2017s122/2017s122(15-07-26).pdf).

### Nominees: internal records and notification

Regulation 6A's nominee arrangement requires all four stated features: the nominee is the registered member under Companies Act section 182; dealings in the shares require the nominator's direction; share rights can only be exercised as directed by the nominator; and the holding is at least **25% plus one share**. Test the actual arrangement, not its label. A holding outside this particular definition does not eliminate the separate beneficial-ownership/control investigation. [Regulation 6A(1)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/proceeds-of-crime/2017s122/2017s122(15-07-26).pdf).

The nominee must hold adequate, accurate and current nominator information. Regulation 6A(2) differentiates natural-person identity particulars, company existence/legal-status/registered-office evidence, equivalent particulars for other arrangements, and the special listed-entity or authorised-regulated-firm particulars. Record the arrangement date. The nominee must notify the company and provide those particulars and reasonably required further information **within 15 days of entering the arrangement**. [Regulation 6A(2)–(4)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/proceeds-of-crime/2017s122/2017s122(15-07-26).pdf).

After submission, material changes or discovered significant inaccuracies must be reported to the company **within 15 days beginning with the change/discovery**, or a later time agreed with the company, with the event date stated. Termination or ceasing to satisfy the regulation's nominee definition also requires company notification **within 15 days**, stating the termination/cessation date. The company then has its own Registrar obligations under regulation 8B; an internal notice is not a filing receipt. Historic arrangements have a separate commencement-based transition, not a new grace period each year. [Regulations 6A(4)–(6), 8B](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/proceeds-of-crime/2017s122/2017s122(15-07-26).pdf).

### Director appointors: identify everyone the rule specifies

Regulation 6B requires the director to obtain and hold the prescribed information about the appointor(s), including identity/entity particulars and the appointment date. Use the actual appointment mechanism:

| Appointment mechanism | Appointors under regulation 6B(2) |
|---|---|
| Named in the stated proposed-officers statement | Subscribers. The formation cross-reference issue identified above still needs resolution. |
| Board appointment | All directors at the appointment time, including absentees and those who did not support it. |
| Appointment by member resolution | All members entitled to vote on the appointment, regardless of attendance or support. |
| Constitutional appointment by majority holders of a share class | All holders of that class at appointment, regardless of attendance or support. |
| Other constitutional right, limb (e) | The person(s) exercising that right where limbs (b)–(d) do not apply. |
| Contractual right, limb (f) | The person(s) exercising that right where limbs (b)–(e) do not apply. |
| Person engaging the director to represent their interests without a contractual or constitutional appointment right | That person **in addition** to the appointors identified under the preceding limbs. |

Source: [regulation 6B(1)–(2)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/proceeds-of-crime/2017s122/2017s122(15-07-26).pdf). For example, a five-member board appoints a director with three votes in favour, one against and one absent. All **five** directors are appointors for this limb; reporting only the three supporters is incorrect. Being an appointor under this rule does not itself attribute control or influence over the director or company: regulation 6B(8) expressly preserves that distinction.

The director supplies the information and reasonably required supporting particulars to the company **within 15 days of appointment**. A discovered significant inaccuracy requires correction **within 15 days beginning with discovery**, unless a later time is agreed with the company, and the discovery date must be stated. Regulation 6B(5) addresses inaccuracies; do not silently restate it as the nominee rule's broader material-change trigger. Appointments include **reappointments**. Regulation 6B(7) excludes directors appointed before that regulation commenced; assess later reappointments separately. The company has its own regulation 8C Registrar obligations, including the linked exception in regulation 8C(6). [Regulation 6B(3)–(8); regulation 8C](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/proceeds-of-crime/2017s122/2017s122(15-07-26).pdf).

Keep the appointment instrument, voting-entitlement/board snapshot, any separate engagement, identity evidence and both internal and Registrar receipts. These ownership-regulation notifications are distinct from Companies House's officer-particulars filing. Historical commencement cases, access restrictions, enforcement and portal-specific verification requirements remain open.

### Current submission route

The GFSC assumed the Registrar function on **1 September 2026**. Its announcement says the old UBO submissions portal closed on **31 August 2026**. GFSC-regulated entities use the UBO section of their GFSC Portal; other covered corporate/legal entities register at [the GFSC UBO portal](https://portal.fsc.gi/uboregister). The announced migration period ended **30 September 2026**; it is not a fresh extension for an October filing. [GFSC announcement, 4 September 2026](https://www.fsc.gi/news/gfsc-assumes-responsibility-for-gibraltars-ultimate-beneficial-ownership-register-590).

Verify account authority, required attachments and successful receipt before treating a filing as complete. No login, submission or migration was performed for this Guide. Access restrictions, additional exceptions, discrepancy reporting, sanctions and historical/portal-specific nominee cases remain open; these core duties do not complete the entire ownership-transparency regime.

## Completion and handover

Registration produces a certificate recording the company name, number, date and type. The statutory effects begin on incorporation, not merely on submitting papers. Keep the certificate, filed constitution, subscriber allocations, consents, compliance statement and filing receipts together. [Companies Act, sections 14–16](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/companies/2014-19/2014-19(01-08-25).pdf).

Complete the ownership-reporting checks above and arrange a separate assessment of business and sector permissions, tax registration and employment requirements. This Guide does not establish completion of those processes.
