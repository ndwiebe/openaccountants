# Kosovo corporate income tax v0.2

Tax year **2026**, checked **24 October 2026**. Source-cited draft, tier 2;
no licensed accountant has attested to this revision.

## Who this applies to

This source-cited draft covers ordinary corporations and other business enterprises with legal personality under Law 06/L-105, Article 4. Resident taxpayers are taxed on Kosovo and foreign-source taxable income; non-residents on Kosovo-source income. Entity residence includes establishment in Kosovo under Article 3(1.13.2). A foreign business needs a separate permanent-establishment assessment under Article 30. Do not apply the company's calculation to a sole proprietor without first determining the correct taxpayer regime. [Articles 3–5][law]

The current Gazette record lists Law 06/L-105 and its repeal of 05/L-029,
without a later amending act listed. TAK's current overview identifies
06/L-105 as the corporate law. Both were read on 24 October 2026. This is
evidence about those current official records, not a conclusion from a missing
search result that no special rule can exist. The older 2016 corporate
instruction implements the repealed statute; its continuing application and
the reporting rules for construction, leasing and gross interest remain open.
The pack therefore keeps IT-CORP partial. [Current Gazette record][record];
[TAK current overview][overview].

## Rates, bands and thresholds

| Test or payment | Rule | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Real-income corporate tax | 10% of taxable income | [06/L-105 art. 7][law] |
| Mandatory real-income regime | Annual gross turnover exceeds EUR 30,000 | [Arts. 35 and 38(3)][law] |
| Small-company trade, transport, agriculture and similar activities | 3% of quarterly gross income; minimum EUR 37.50 for an income-bearing quarter | [Art. 38(2.1.1)][law] |
| Small-company services, professional, vocational, entertainment and similar activities | 9% of quarterly gross income; minimum EUR 37.50 for an income-bearing quarter | [Art. 38(2.1.2)][law] |
| Small-company rent | 10% of net rent, less relevant withholding | [Art. 38(2.1.3)][law] |
| Quarter with no income | No payment, but quarterly statement still required | [Art. 38(2.1.4)][law] |
| Ordinary representation | Maximum 1% of annual gross income | [Art. 12(2)][law] |
| Qualifying public-interest donations | Ordinary ceiling 10% of taxable income before the donation deduction | [Art. 11][law] |
| Depreciation, categories 1 / 2 / 3 | 5% / 20% / 10% straight line | [Art. 16(3)–(4)][law] |
| Low-value asset | Up to EUR 1,000, subject to functional-group/life exception | [Art. 16(7)][law] |
| Qualifying new assets | Additional 10% cost deduction in first-use year, subject to eligibility | [Art. 18][law] |
| Undefined-life intangible | Straight-line over 20 years | [Art. 20(3)][law] |
| Loss carryforward | Four successive periods, oldest first | [Art. 24][law] |

[Law][law]; [TAKS current overview][overview]. Exactly EUR 30,000 is within the small-company ceiling if no election or continuing real-income obligation applies. Do not describe the small regime as no corporate-tax liability: it substitutes payments based on gross income for the ordinary profit calculation. Rental income has a separate net-rent computation and withholding credit under Article 38(2.1.3); do not apply the trade or service percentage to it.

### Election and threshold crossing

A qualifying small taxpayer can elect the real-income books/records regime. An existing taxpayer applies before 1 March of the chosen tax period; a new taxpayer's first quarterly statement communicates the election. The election year and at least three following tax periods are covered. Crossing EUR 30,000 similarly brings the crossing year and at least three following periods into real-income treatment. [Articles 35(2)–(3), 38(3)][law]

Returning to small-business books requires the statutory conditions and TAK
approval **before** 1 March. Article 35(4)'s English wording refers to income
in the last quarter being below the limit. Retain TAK's approval and the
turnover/election history; a later low-turnover year alone does not cancel
the continuing obligation. The exact administrative application of that
quarter wording remains on the unconfirmed list. [Article 35(4)][law]

## The base

For real-income taxpayers, begin with financial-statement profit and reconcile each adjustment to the supporting ledger and statutory provision. Gross income less allowable deductions determines taxable income under Article 6(1). Article 6(2) separately addresses long-term construction, leasing and gross interest, with reporting delegated to implementing rules; do not infer their full computation from the ordinary bridge below. [Articles 6 and 37][law]

1. Identify exempt income separately. Article 8 includes dividends paid or received by resident and non-resident persons. Do not extend this to interest, royalties or capital gains.
2. Add back corporate income tax, public-authority fines/penalties and related interest, recoverable input VAT, and capital costs that require separate treatment. [Article 9][law]
3. Deduct documented costs incurred wholly and exclusively for economic activity, subject to the statutory limits. For an accrued expense subject to withholding, payment after 31 March of the following period postpones deduction to its payment period. [Article 10][law]
4. Replace accounting depreciation with the supported tax schedule. Test losses before using them.
5. Apply 10% and reconcile allowable withholding, instalment and foreign-tax credits to evidence. Do not deduct the same credit twice. [Articles 7, 29 and 38][law]

Representation is limited to 1% of annual gross income; ordinary media
advertising/promotion is separately deductible under art. 12. Public-interest
contributions require the eligible recipient, payment receipt and absence of
direct or indirect donor/related-person benefit under art. 11. The ordinary
10% limit is measured before the contribution deduction. Special sponsorship
relief requires its own law and approval evidence, rather than treating every
donation as a tax credit. [Articles 11–12][law]

Bad debt requires previously recognised income, an undisputed debt, collection
efforts, more than six months overdue and initiation of the declaration
procedures within 24 months of the payment obligation. Judicial proceedings
are not required for amounts at most EUR 500. Related-party debts and repeated
transactions after declaring the debt bad are excluded, with the specified
public-service exception. Deduct only the unrecovered amount; a later recovery
is taxable. An unsupported ledger write-off is insufficient. [Article 13][law]

Related-party interest, rent and other charges are deductible at the **lower
of actual payment and open-market amount**, rather than increasing an expense
to market value when less was paid. Compensation/wages have their open-market
test. [Article 15][law]

Employee education requires direct payment to a recognised institution,
relevance to the existing job, no qualification for a different occupation,
and the prescribed post-education employment period. The latter implementing
period remains to be confirmed. Job-related training has the separate
deduction under art. 10(3). Non-business property rent can use a 10% deemed
expense if actual-cost records are not maintained; the property-letting
business is excluded from that option. [Article 10][law]

### Depreciation

Article 16 provides individual straight-line depreciation: buildings/structures 5%; Category 2 assets including vehicles, computers and office equipment 20%; Category 3 plant/machinery and other specified assets 10%. Begin when the asset is first put into service. Land, art and other non-wasting property are excluded. Cost includes the specified pre-service charges and supplier incidentals. [Article 16(1)–(6)][law]

An asset costing EUR 1,000 or less can be a current expense, except where it
forms part of a functioning asset group whose total value exceeds EUR 1,000
and life exceeds one year. Document the functional unit rather than divide a
single system into invoices. Leasehold improvements use straight-line
depreciation over the lease life. [Articles 3(1.1), 16][law]

Article 18 adds a 10% acquisition-cost deduction in the first-use year for
the listed production lines, machinery, railway assets, aircraft, ships and
heavy vehicles/equipment. The asset must be new or first placed in use in
Kosovo, and not transferred from an existing/former business inside **or
outside** Kosovo. It is unavailable to persons benefiting under art. 8(3).
This condition differs from PIT art. 22; do not copy the individual rule into
the company calculation. [Article 18][law]

Non-routine repairs/improvements extending life at least one year and costing
more than EUR 1,000 per asset are capitalised. Amounts at most EUR 1,000 are
expensed; ordinary maintenance is separate. Limited-life intangibles amortise
straight-line over contractual useful life; an undefined life uses 20 years.
Biological-asset and natural-resource calculations require the additional
rules in arts. 17 and 21. [Articles 17–21][law]

### Disposals, distributions and cessation

Capital gain is proceeds plus other consideration less acquisition cost,
improvements and prior allowed depreciation. Adjust related-party undervalue
to market. Gains and losses are ordinary business items; the four-year loss
rule applies. Instalment-sale gains are spread straight-line over the
enforceable agreement. Involuntary conversion has the same-character
replacement condition and two-year period in arts. 22–23. [Articles 22–24][law]

Non-share property distributed to a shareholder triggers company gain/loss
as an open-market sale. Liquidation similarly values distributed property at
market, with the separate recipient and parent-company rules in art. 26.
A qualifying reorganisation needs a written plan approved by TAK; carryover
basis and succession apply under art. 27, and loss transfer needs the plan's
express provision and approval. Do not assume that a dividend exemption
eliminates the distributing company's asset gain. These are positive disposal
rules, not proof of either a universal departure charge or its absence.
[Articles 25–27][law]

### Losses

Carry a tax loss forward for up to four successive tax periods, oldest first, reduced by amounts already used. Except for the Article 27 reorganisation rules, the loss belongs to the business that incurred it. A change in business-organisation type or ownership change of **more than 50%** prevents transfer under Article 24(5). Exactly 50% does not meet that particular ownership trigger; other restrictions still require testing. Keep loss-year, expiry, utilisation and ownership evidence. [Article 24][law]

## Deadlines and filing

The ordinary tax period is the calendar year. Quarterly advances are due on or before 15 April, 15 July, 15 October and 15 January for the preceding quarter. Real-income taxpayers submit the annual declaration/financial statements and settle by 31 March following the tax period. A business closure has a separate declaration timing rule. For an ordinary 2026 calendar year, the final quarter payment is due 15 January 2027 and annual settlement 31 March 2027; check any official non-working-day or extension rule before execution. [Articles 3(1.19), 37–38][law]

For real-income advances, Article 38(2.2) permits one quarter of estimated current-year tax, less relevant withholding for the quarter. From the second period, the alternative is at least one quarter of 110% of prior-period liability, less relevant quarterly withholding. A prior-year loss excludes the prior-liability method. Instalments are credits against the annual result, not additional tax. [Article 38(2.2), (5), (7.4)][law]

Retain sales/purchase books, cash records, asset schedules and financial
statements under art. 35; small-company records remain required under art. 36.
The current TAK timetable identifies QS for small companies, QL for real-income
advances and CD for the annual corporate return. The CD layout includes
foreign-tax, withholding and instalment credits separately. File using the
current EDI form, not a guessed box number. In a closure, submit the annual
statement in the period when applying for business deregistration, under
art. 37(5). [Articles 35–38][law]; [TAK timetable][overview]; [CD layout][cd].

## Penalties and interest

Article 38(7) addresses late or insufficient advances using the interest rate
in force when payment was due, charged on the underpaid amount for the specified
period. Its relief tests include timely filing/payment conditions, the 20%
instalment difference test, prior-year liability protection, the audit-adjustment
exception and the first-year cumulative payment rule. Do not treat 110% of
prior-year liability as an unconditional waiver. [Article 38(7)][law]

Current procedure Law 08/L-257 art. 99 charges a legal entity EUR 150 for each
missing mandatory return resulting in tax liability. Article 100 charges 15%
of the understatement difference where at most 10% of correct tax, and 25%
where larger. Failure to withhold/pay tax or pension has a separate 25% of
shortfall under art. 103. Apply the distinct triggers and statutory exclusions
rather than automatically adding every penalty. [Arts. 99–103][procedure]

Late interest runs for each month or part-month, at most ten years, at the
Minister's rate published by TAK. A compliant instalment agreement stops it
from the following month; default reinstates it. The numeric 2026 tax-interest
rate is not verified here. Do not substitute the repealed procedure statute's
penalty schedule or a commercial-debt reference rate. [Art. 24][procedure]

## Cross-border points

Controlled transactions require arm's-length profit and documentation under Article 28. Article 29 provides a foreign-tax credit limited to the Kosovo tax attributable to the foreign income and gives applicable treaties precedence. Articles 31–33 contain separate withholding obligations. Treaty eligibility, current treaty rates, permanent establishments and detailed transfer-pricing procedures need their own verified workpapers; none is marked covered by this Guide. [Articles 28–33][law]

## Two worked examples that recompute

### 1. Ordinary profit and annual settlement

Assume a resident company in the real-income regime, no exempt/special income, accounting profit EUR 40,000 after a EUR 2,000 non-deductible public fine and EUR 8,000 accounting depreciation. Independently verified tax depreciation is EUR 10,000. A EUR 5,000 carried loss survives all conditions. The tax base is EUR 35,000, tax EUR 3,500 and balance EUR 900 after EUR 2,400 advances and EUR 200 allowable withholding credit. Depreciation and loss eligibility are case assumptions, not inferred from the arithmetic.

```oa-check
name: Kosovo corporate profit and settlement bridge
inputs:
  profit: 40000
  fine: 2000
  book_depreciation: 8000
  tax_depreciation: 10000
  eligible_loss: 5000
  tax_rate: 0.10
  advances: 2400
  withholding_credit: 200
steps:
  before_losses: profit + fine + book_depreciation - tax_depreciation
  taxable: before_losses - eligible_loss
  tax: taxable * tax_rate
  balance: tax - advances - withholding_credit
expected:
  before_losses: 40000
  taxable: 35000
  tax: 3500
  balance: 900
```

### 2. Small-service-company quarterly minimum

Assume an eligible service company with annual gross income no higher than EUR 30,000, no prior real-income obligation/election, and no rental or mixed activities. Quarterly receipts EUR 200, 5,000, zero and 6,000 yield payments EUR 37.50, 450, zero and 540. The zero quarter still requires its statement. These are gross-income payments: expenses do not reduce them.

```oa-check
name: Kosovo small service company quarterly payments
inputs:
  q1: 200
  q2: 5000
  q3: 0
  q4: 6000
  rate: 0.09
  minimum: 37.50
steps:
  annual_gross: q1 + q2 + q3 + q4
  payment_q1: max(minimum, q1 * rate)
  payment_q2: max(minimum, q2 * rate)
  payment_q3: 0
  payment_q4: max(minimum, q4 * rate)
  total: payment_q1 + payment_q2 + payment_q3 + payment_q4
expected:
  annual_gross: 11200
  payment_q1: 37.50
  payment_q2: 450
  payment_q3: 0
  payment_q4: 540
  total: 1027.50
```

## Ask the client first

- What is the legal entity, place of establishment and foreign presence?
- What are annual and quarterly gross receipts by activity, including rental income?
- Was real-income taxation elected or triggered in any of the preceding periods?
- Are accounts, asset-service dates, loss history and ownership changes documented?
- Which accrued costs involve withholding, and when were they paid?
- What advances, withholding and foreign taxes were actually paid and evidenced?
